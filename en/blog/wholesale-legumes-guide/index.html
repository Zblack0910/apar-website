Wholesale Legumes: A Buyer's Guide

Iran is one of the world's largest producers and exporters of legumes — including beans, chickpeas, lentils, and split peas. For international buyers, Iran offers an attractive combination of high-quality produce, competitive pricing, and reliable supply. This guide walks you through everything you need to know about importing wholesale legumes from Iran.

Iran's Legume Production Landscape

Iran's diverse climate — ranging from the temperate Caspian region to the arid central plateaus — allows for the cultivation of a wide variety of legumes. Key growing regions include Khorasan, Kermanshah, Lorestan, and Kurdistan provinces, each known for specific varieties.

The country produces an estimated 1+ million metric tons of legumes annually, with significant export capacity to markets across the Middle East, Europe, and Asia.

Popular Export Varieties

Beans

Iranian beans — particularly pinto beans, white beans (navy/great northern), and red kidney beans — are valued for their uniform size, rich flavor, and excellent cooking properties. They are popular in canned bean production and retail markets worldwide.

Chickpeas

Iran produces both Kabuli (large, light-colored) and Desi (smaller, darker) chickpea varieties. Kabuli chickpeas from Iran are especially prized for their large size, creamy texture, and are a staple in Middle Eastern, Indian, and European cuisines.

Lentils

Green and red/brown lentils from Iran are known for their quick cooking times and rich, earthy flavors. They are a major export commodity to countries where lentil-based soups and dishes are dietary staples.

Split Peas

Yellow and green split peas are primarily used in processed food manufacturing, soups, and purees. Iranian split peas are valued for their consistent split quality and low foreign matter content.

Quality Grading & Standards

Understanding quality grading is crucial when sourcing legumes at wholesale. Key quality indicators include:

Size Uniformity

Premium legumes are sorted by size using calibrated screens. Consistent sizing ensures even cooking, attractive presentation, and meets the specifications of retail and food-service buyers.

Moisture Content

Optimal moisture content for storage and export is between 12–14%. Too high, and the product risks mold; too low, and the beans may become brittle during shipping.

Foreign Matter & Defects

Top-grade legumes have minimal foreign matter (stones, stems, insects) and very low percentages of broken, discolored, or insect-damaged kernels. Reputable exporters like APAR run multiple cleaning and sorting passes to achieve these standards.

Packaging Options

  • PP Bags (25–50 kg): Standard for wholesale and industrial buyers
  • Small Retail Packs (500g – 2 kg): For supermarket distribution
  • Vacuum Packs: Extended shelf life for premium retail markets
  • Custom Packaging: Private label options with your branding and specifications

Shipping & Documentation

Legume exports from Iran require proper documentation for customs clearance in the destination country. Essential documents include:

  • Phytosanitary Certificate: Issued by Iran's Plant Protection Organization
  • Health Certificate: Confirming the product meets food safety standards
  • Quality Certificate: Lab analysis report for the specific batch
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill: Shipping documentation
  • Commercial Invoice & Packing List: Standard trade documents

For shipping, legumes are typically loaded in 20ft containers (approximately 18–22 MT) or 40ft containers (up to 26 MT). Container selection depends on volume requirements and destination port capabilities.

Selecting a Supplier

When choosing a legume supplier in Iran, consider:

  • Direct sourcing capabilities (farm-to-port supply chain)
  • In-house quality control and sorting facilities
  • Experience with your target market's specifications
  • Ability to provide all required export documentation
  • Transparent pricing and flexible payment terms
